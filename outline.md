# Corporate Finance with AI — Outline

15 chapters, three acts of 3-5-7. See `l3-arc.md` for the full arc and transition logic.

## Act 1 — Read the Firm

**Ch 1. The CFO's First Question** *(drafted 2026-04-27)*
Maya's first week. The CFO asks her to evaluate $50M debt vs equity for a plant expansion. The chapter introduces the inside-the-firm perspective, the three-beat method rehearsed for CFO contexts, and the verification toolkit. Onboarding load chapter — must work for Book 1 returnees and fresh starters.

**Ch 2. Reading the Firm from Inside** *(drafted 2026-04-27)*
What a CFO sees when she opens the books that an outside analyst doesn't. Operating accruals, working capital signals, the divergence between reported numbers and operational cash reality. Specifies what "reading the firm" actually means.

**Ch 3. Working Capital Is Where the Cash Lives** *(drafted 2026-04-27)*
Cash conversion cycle as the daily reality. Days sales outstanding, days payable outstanding, days inventory outstanding. How working capital decisions free up internal capital — bridges to Act 2's allocation question.

## Act 2 — Allocate Capital

**Ch 4. Capital Budgeting at the Firm Level** *(drafted 2026-04-27)*
NPV when the project is yours, not a public-company comp. Cash flow projection from operations. Sunk costs, opportunity costs, terminal values. Maya runs the plant expansion analysis from Ch 1.

**Ch 5. The Cost of Capital and the WACC** *(drafted 2026-04-27)*
Specifies what "cost of capital" means and why WACC is the discount rate that links investment and financing. The CAPM as one input. The component-cost mechanics, then weighting.

**Ch 6. Risk-Adjusted Rates and Real Options** *(drafted 2026-04-27)*
Why one WACC isn't enough. Project-specific risk. The optionality NPV ignores: defer, expand, abandon. When traditional DCF understates value.

**Ch 7. Capital Structure Theory: The Modigliani-Miller World** *(drafted 2026-04-27)*
The two MM propositions as a baseline. Force the student to use MM as a measuring stick — quantify the tax shield within Ch 7 itself. **Paired with Ch 8.**

**Ch 8. Capital Structure in the Real World** *(drafted 2026-04-27)*
The frictions that make MM-irrelevance fail: taxes, distress costs, asymmetric information, agency problems. The trade-off and pecking-order theories. Maya recommends a capital structure change for Halverson with quantified value impact. **Paired with Ch 7.**

## Act 3 — Distribute and Execute

**Ch 9. Returning Capital: Dividends, Buybacks, and the Choice Between Them** *(drafted 2026-04-27)*
Payout policy. Why two firms with identical operations can pay out cash so differently. Signaling, taxes, clientele effects.

**Ch 10. Raising Capital: IPOs, Secondaries, and the Cost of Going to Market** *(drafted 2026-04-27)*
The mechanics of equity issuance. Underwriting fees, underpricing, dilution, the lockup. When a CFO reaches for external capital and what it costs.

**Ch 11. M&A: The Largest Decisions a CFO Makes** *(drafted 2026-04-27)*
Strategic logic, valuation under deal pressure, financing the deal, integration risk. The empirical record on whether acquisitions create value (and for whom).

**Ch 12. Operational Risk Management** *(drafted 2026-04-27)*
Hedging interest rate, FX, and commodity exposure. When to hedge, when to bear the risk, what derivatives actually cost.

**Ch 13. International Corporate Finance** *(drafted 2026-04-27)*
Cross-border operations as a layer of complexity, not a separate discipline. Currency exposure, country risk, transfer pricing, repatriation.

**Ch 14. Behavioral Corporate Finance** *(drafted 2026-04-27)*
Where rigorous analysis goes wrong because the CFO is human. Overconfidence, anchoring, escalation of commitment. Embedded checks throughout the verification chain.

**Ch 15. The Capstone: An Integrated CFO Recommendation** *(drafted 2026-04-27)*
Maya synthesizes investment, financing, and payout into one coherent recommendation. Verification chain explicit, behavioral risks named, defensible to a board.
